Privacy Policy for WARN

Last updated: September 2026

WARN (Wildlife Alert and Road-hazard Network) is a road-safety app that warns drivers about wildlife and road hazards reported by other drivers nearby. This policy explains what the app collects, what leaves your device, and what it is used for.

No accounts, no sign-in

WARN has no user accounts, no login, no email address, and no password. You are not asked who you are, and the app has no way to know. Nothing in this policy depends on you identifying yourself, because you never do.

How you are identified — you aren't

This is worth stating precisely, because most apps cannot say it. A hazard report submitted by a public user carries no identifier of any kind. What is stored is the hazard's category, its description, its coordinates, the direction of travel, the region, and the time. There is no account, no user ID, no device ID, and no random per-install token attached to a report — not even an anonymous one. The database has no column in which your identity could be recorded, so reports cannot be grouped by who filed them, by us or by anyone else.

Fleet and municipal users are different, and deliberately so: those roles authenticate in order to post notices only their own organisation can see, using daily-rotating credentials rather than persistent personal accounts.

Location

Where your device is, moment to moment. WARN needs your location continuously while it is running, because its entire function is to warn you about hazards ahead of you on the road. This runs as a foreground service, so Android shows a persistent notification whenever it is active. This continuous location is used in real time to decide what to alert you about, and is not recorded, uploaded, or kept as a trail. WARN does not build a history of where you have driven, and cannot reconstruct your route.

Where a hazard is. This is the important exception, and we want to be plain about it: when you report a hazard, the hazard's coordinates are your device's coordinates at that moment, and those coordinates are published — they appear on the map and in the list for every other user in the area. A hazard report is a public statement that something was seen at a particular place at a particular time. It is attached only to that day's random identifier, never to your name, but it is not private.

Hazard reports

A report consists of the hazard's category, a short description, its coordinates, the direction of travel, and the time. All of this is public to other users by design — a hazard nobody else can see would not warn anyone. Reports expire automatically after a period that depends on the hazard type.

WARN does not take or attach photographs, and does not request camera access.

Voice reporting and the microphone

WARN is designed to be used hands-free while driving, so hazards can be reported by voice. This is worth explaining carefully, because it is the part of the app that involves the most outside processing.

  • The microphone is active only while you are making a report — after you press the report control. WARN does not listen continuously, does not use a wake word, and is not recording in the background.
  • Your speech is transcribed to text using Android's built-in speech recognition, which in normal operation sends the audio to Google for transcription over the network. Some devices can do this offline, but WARN does not rely on that and most devices do not have it available. Google's handling of that audio is governed by Google's own privacy policy.
  • The resulting text is then sent for classification to Google's Gemini service, reached through a small relay on our own Supabase backend rather than called directly from your phone. This is what lets you say "big moose just past the bridge" in your own words instead of learning a fixed vocabulary. What is sent is the words of your report — not your coordinates, not the audio, and no identifier, because there isn't one to send.
  • WARN does not store the audio. It is not saved on your device and is never uploaded to WARN's own servers. What is kept is the resulting hazard report, exactly as described above.

The same Gemini service is also used to translate hazard descriptions and app labels into your chosen language. Only the text being translated is sent.

IP addresses

One thing does get recorded, and we would rather name it plainly than leave it out. Because WARN has no accounts, the only way to stop somebody scripting abuse of the classification relay described above is to count requests by network address. When your phone uses that relay, its IP address is stored briefly in a counter table so we can tell whether one source is making an unreasonable number of requests.

That counter holds nothing but the address, a timestamp, and a number. It is not linked to hazard reports — the reports have no identifier to link it to — and rows are discarded shortly after their time window ends. It is not used to identify anyone, to build a profile, or to track a device between sessions.

Fleet vehicles

WARN can be installed on vehicles operated by a company or agency — for example, taxi or utility fleets — where it may run on a shared device in kiosk mode under a single account belonging to the operator, not to any individual driver. In that configuration the operator can post hazards and notices visible only to their own vehicles.

Because such devices are shared and carry no personal account, WARN cannot tell which driver is using a fleet device, and does not report driver identity, routes, speed, or behaviour to the operator or to anyone else. WARN is a hazard-warning system, not a driver-monitoring system, and is not designed to be used as one.

Where the data goes

Hazard data is stored on Supabase, which provides the database and backend hosting. Speech transcription and hazard classification involve Google services as described above. These providers process data on our behalf and have their own privacy policies.

Retention

Hazard reports are deleted outright when they expire — they are removed from the database, not merely hidden from the map. How long that takes depends on the hazard: wildlife reports last roughly two hours in daylight and busy periods and up to six overnight, resetting whenever another driver confirms the sighting, while other categories have their own timers.

If a long-term record of expired hazards is added in future — public agencies are often legally required to retain records of hazards and closures — this policy will be updated before that happens. No such archive exists today.

What WARN does not do

  • No advertising, and no sale or sharing of data with advertisers or data brokers.
  • No tracking of individuals, and no profile of any driver.
  • No reporting of police activity or speed traps. WARN is a safety system; this is a deliberate design decision, not an oversight.
  • No collection of contacts, messages, photographs, installed apps, or browsing activity, and no camera access at all.

Your choices

  • Location and microphone permissions can be denied or revoked at any time in your device's settings. WARN cannot warn you about nearby hazards without location, and cannot take voice reports without the microphone, but denying either does not prevent the rest of the app from working.
  • You can request removal of a hazard report by contacting us below. Please include roughly when and where it was reported — since reports carry no identifier, that is the only way we can find the right one.

Children's privacy

WARN is intended for drivers and is not directed at children. It does not knowingly collect information from children.
